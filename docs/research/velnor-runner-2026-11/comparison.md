# Comparison of eight proposals

Eight models answered the same panel brief (v1, 2026-11-18) with the same evidence pack (facts measured 2026-11-18). Each worked alone and saw no other answer. This document sets the eight answers side by side and adds no design of its own. In a second round each model reviewed three anonymised proposals from other vendors; the last three sections report that round, and statements in the earlier sections that the reviews settled or corrected carry a note beginning "Reviews:" (in the numbers table, a sentence in the last column). The panel, with ids: Claude Opus 5.5 (`opus-5.5`), Claude Fable 5.2 (`fable-5.2`), GPT-6.2-Astra (`gpt-6.2-astra`), GPT-6.2-Sol (`gpt-6.2-sol`), Muse Wonder (`muse-wonder-1.0`), Muse Wonder Full (`muse-wonder-full-1.0`), Gemini 4 Carbon (`gemini-4-carbon`) and Gemini 4 Argon (`gemini-4-argon`). Cells quote or closely paraphrase the proposals. "—" means the model is silent on the point. "MG" is a `merge_group` run. "Evidence §A T5" names a section and table of the evidence pack. A number is marked measured, from the evidence pack, or estimated wherever that changes how far it can be trusted.

## Hypotheses

Verdict words are the models' own, as first submitted; "Hypotheses after the reviews" lists what changed. H1: Rust-native actions and runner are dramatically faster. H2: a from-scratch Rust runner beats wrapping the official runner and ARC. H3: Kubernetes is the right base. H4: local, placement-aware caches beat remote ones. H5: workflows can run only what a change needs.

| | Claude Opus 5.5 | Claude Fable 5.2 | GPT-6.2-Astra | GPT-6.2-Sol | Muse Wonder | Muse Wonder Full | Gemini 4 Carbon | Gemini 4 Argon |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | **fails** — JS steps 17 s per job, network-bound; locality, not language | **fails** — interpreter is about 0.6% of job seconds; the rest is network | **fails** — three action steps total about 6 s, roughly 1% of path | **fails** — action steps about 9 s, under 2% of a 494 s job | **partly** — true for cache and checkout locality; job time sits in cargo | **fails** — deleting every JS action saves under 15 s, about 3% | **fails** — Node and C# overhead is 2.0–2.5 s per job | **fails** — Node startup and execution 3–5 s per job, about 1% |
| H2 | **partly** — build the dispatcher; native executor adds only 2–3 s per hop | **partly** — replace ARC and listener; Worker last, about 20 s per run | **unproven** — needs ≥30 s or ≥10% on a matched warm workload | **unproven** — at most 39 s from three dispatches; no paired data | **fails** — legacy's breadth (804k lines) is the counter-evidence; holds for restricted profile | **partly / unproven** — controller yes, "ARC must go"; native executor unproven as first move | **partly** — replacing ARC wins 10–16 s; replacing the Worker 2.5–4.5 s | **partly** — holds for ARC; fails for the Worker (under 0.5 s) |

| H4 | **holds** — zero cross-job reuse today; ≥95% dependency hits expected | **holds** — strongest; measured warm build 8.8 s against 22.5 s cold | **partly** — a cold node gains nothing from locality alone | **holds** — as a hybrid: node-local plus a shared per-site miss path | **holds** — strongest; cross-job hit rate is zero where it matters | **holds** — strongest; local NVMe about 50× faster than the remote cache | **holds** — reflink clone about 1 s against 44–61 s remote restore | **holds** — warm mbx build 15.5 s against 72.5 s cold (project doc) |
| H5 | **holds** — largest lever after queueing; needs per-job read verification | **holds in principle** — not delivered today: heavy jobs ran in 97.4% of PR runs | **partly** — unknown reads, external inputs and nondeterministic checks still require execution | **holds** — planner already skips 14 of 39; long tests must shrink | **holds** — second-largest and safest lever; merge_group and push ignore selection | **holds** — under-exploited: selection is off exactly on the merge path | **holds** — largest absolute drop in push-to-merge and cluster load | **holds** — 5–15× from topology, selection, consolidation, sharding and deduplication |

- **H1: consensus.** Seven say fails, Muse Wonder says partly, and all eight give the same reason: the seconds to tens of seconds per job that can be saved come from locality (local git, tools, action tarballs, caches), in any language. The evidence pack supports "fails as stated": action and setup steps take seconds inside jobs of 67–508 s (§A T9b, T10), the runner already forces every JavaScript action to node24 (§F 5.3), and velnor-legacy recorded "no performance improvement accepted yet" (§E 7).
- **H2: split.** Five say partly, two unproven, one fails. All eight want Velnor-owned placement and cache services; the split is over the step executor (and, for GPT-6.2-Astra, over whether ARC's control loop needs replacing at all). The pack supports "unproven" for the executor: it holds no measurement of a native runner against the official one (§E 7), and cluster jobs already start after 0.2 min (§A T4).
- **H3: near consensus on "partly".** Gemini 4 Argon says holds and attaches the same conditions the others list as defects. The pack supports "partly": cluster queue is 0.2 min median (§A T4), yet pods are emptyDir-only with 2-CPU requests (§B 3.3) and job times grow from 3.7 to 8.8 min under load (§A T6).
- **H4: consensus.** Seven say holds; GPT-6.2-Astra says partly. The pack supports the direction: no reuse crosses a job on the scale set (§A T9a, T9b) and the GitHub cache delivers 13.6–22.4 MB/s at 120 ms (§B 4.2). It holds no measurement of a node-local compile-cache hit on the cluster, so the size of the gain is unmeasured.
- **H5: consensus with two qualifiers.** Six say holds, Claude Fable 5.2 "in principle, not delivered", GPT-6.2-Astra partly. The pack supports the duplicate-run part: selection exists on PRs only (§C 4), each merge is verified three times, and 120 of 159 successful MG runs have a push run on the identical SHA (§A T7). How well today's selection works is contested (see Q1).

Agree:
- All eight: the waiting is on GitHub-hosted gate jobs. Cluster jobs queue 0.2 min median (evidence §A T4, T5).
- All eight: no compile result crosses a job on the scale set, and every merge is verified three times (§A T9a, T7).
- A run with no hosted queue takes 10–12 min: Claude Fable 5.2 observed 10.9, GPT-6.2-Astra and GPT-6.2-Sol replayed 11.4 and 12.0, Claude Opus 5.5 sums about 10, Muse Wonder 10–11, Muse Wonder Full 9–11.
- Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Fable 5.2, GPT-6.2-Astra and GPT-6.2-Sol each found that a heavy job's test step lasts as long as its one slowest test (203–274 s in most sampled runs, 324–534 s in the loaded 11-18 runs Claude Fable 5.2 read), so sharding alone cannot shorten it.
- Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Fable 5.2 read the org's plan as Free (20 concurrent hosted jobs); Gemini 4 Carbon cites the same 20-job limit. These three, with Muse Wonder Full and Gemini 4 Argon, name the repo's own Source qualification and Job image candidate runs as competitors for that queue. Reviews: Claude Opus 5.5 read the plan as Free again; Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full hold that the 20-job limit is not shown to be what binds this public repository.
- Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6.2-Sol add that Ledger CLI, which evidence T5's gate chain omits, often heads the critical path (8 of 18 runs in GPT-6.2-Sol's cohort).

Disagree, and conflicts with the evidence pack:
- **Jobs executed per PR run.** Evidence §A T2 and T7 say 39. GPT-6.2-Astra and GPT-6.2-Sol recount 25 executed and 14 skipped; Claude Opus 5.5 counts 17 of 31 cluster jobs. Gemini 4 Argon says the globs make "77 of 77 sampled PR runs" trigger all jobs. Muse Wonder, Muse Wonder Full and Gemini 4 Carbon use 39 (or 31) in their arithmetic. Reviews: Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Fable 5.2 and GPT-6.2-Sol back 25 executed; Muse Wonder calls the difference definitional; Muse Wonder Full finds 17, 25 and 39 never reconciled job by job.
- **Push-to-merge today.** The pack does not measure it. Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Fable 5.2 measured PR timelines; GPT-6.2-Astra and GPT-6.2-Sol used small samples; Gemini 4 Carbon adds run medians (33.8 and 141.2 min); Gemini 4 Argon labels run wall time (26.7–77.8 min) as push-to-merge. Reviews: the timeline method is accepted (Muse Wonder: "Replace every other baseline with it"); its clock starts at the first CI run on the final head, not at the git push.
- **Auto-merge.** Claude Opus 5.5 measured that 0 of 100 PRs enabled it. Claude Fable 5.2 reads the queue-add times as "fits auto-merge".
- **Merge-queue timer.** Gemini 4 Carbon counts 5.0 "dead minutes" per merge. GPT-6.2-Astra and GPT-6.2-Sol say the timer applies only while the minimum group size is unmet, and the minimum is 1 (ruleset in evidence §C 1). Claude Opus 5.5 measured 19 s from enqueue to MG run. Reviews: five reviewers agree there is no fixed floor, and Gemini 4 Carbon withdrew the 5 minutes in its own review.
- **Why the sources cache misses 48 of 48.** GPT-6.2-Astra found that the missing key equals the key Plan hits. GPT-6.2-Sol, Gemini 4 Carbon and Gemini 4 Argon trace the miss to the cache version: `runner.temp` is `/home/runner/work/_temp` on hosted runners and `/home/runner/_work/_temp` on the scale set. Muse Wonder says the key itself contains the platform. Evidence §A T9a gives no cause. The two Gemini models also disagree on the hosted `ImageOS` value (`ubuntu26` against `ubuntu24`). Reviews: Rust jobs take the key from Plan's output, so the match is by construction and Muse Wonder's reading is wrong; three reviewers confirmed the path-derived version in code; the generated script expects `ubuntu26` on `ubuntu-26.04` (Claude Fable 5.2).
- **Toolchain cost.** Evidence §A T9b: "Prepare pinned tools" is 10 s median. Muse Wonder writes "~30–60 s of per-job install". Evidence §B 3.3 says the image has the toolchain baked in; Claude Fable 5.2, Muse Wonder Full, Gemini 4 Carbon and Gemini 4 Argon cite the owner's RESULT.md (line 115): "Not baked in: the Rust toolchain". Reviews: the manager confirmed the RESULT.md reading (see "Evidence pack errata"); Gemini 4 Carbon's review marks GPT-6.2-Sol's "The cluster image already seeds tools" as false on the same ground.
- **Cluster CPU.** Gemini 4 Carbon's headline says the workers "sit near 0% CPU"; Muse Wonder calls the cluster "CPU-saturated". Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Fable 5.2, GPT-6.2-Astra, GPT-6.2-Sol and Muse Wonder Full describe a bursty, unbalanced load (samples from 0.35% to 95%, minutes apart or across workers), as do the three samples in evidence §B 2.1.
- **Where emptyDir lives.** Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Fable 5.2, Gemini 4 Carbon and Gemini 4 Argon say on the single OS disk. Evidence §B 1.4 says "Unknown"; GPT-6.2-Sol and Muse Wonder Full say it cannot be identified read-only. Reviews split the same way: four reviewers treat the OS-disk placement as verified (`PLAN.md:45`, nodefs usage), two as unproven.
- **State-producer failures.** Evidence §A (discrepancies table): 7 at the tip check, 9 at Clippy or the parallel-check wait. Gemini 4 Argon attributes all 9 to an `event.json` truncation race, from one job log. No other model reports this. Reviews: Claude Fable 5.2 and Muse Wonder Full verified the race mechanism in the runner and helper code; all three reviewers say one log cannot assign all 9 failures to it.

### Q3. Runner architecture

| Model | Key position |
|---|---|
| Claude Opus 5.5 | Rust `velnor-dispatch` and `velnor-node` own placement, warm sandboxes and trust-scoped mounts. The official Worker executes. Journal kept. Native executor optional. |
| Claude Fable 5.2 | Dispatcher and node agent with slots; crash-only, no journal. Executors L0, L1, L2 with fallback. Line budgets set before coding. |
| GPT-6.2-Astra | Two interfaces: GitHub-facing job lifecycle and machine-facing admission. Official worker is executor and oracle. Reuse velnor-new's journal, proofs and PR #1324's runtime boundary. |
| GPT-6.2-Sol | Choose the scheduler independently of the executor. Compare three routes on separate scale sets. Journaled ownership with fencing epochs. Delay the general runtime trait. |
| Muse Wonder | Three layers: Velnor listener and scheduler (replaces ARC), unmodified official runner, new node platform agent. A fast lane for one profile. |
| Muse Wonder Full | One controller, two lanes, fail-safe `admit()`. Evolve `velnor-runner-host` behind a `Runtime` trait. Plan and verdict become controller-native computations. |
| Gemini 4 Carbon | `velnor-coordinator`, `velnor-node` and a native `velnor-worker`. Rootless namespaces and KVM microVMs. Replace the 171k-line host and its journal. |
| Gemini 4 Argon | `velnor-host` with a `KubernetesRuntime` (PR #1324) and `velnor-node-agent`. Unmodified runner in standby pods. Turso journal kept. Slow WAN paths intercepted. |

Agree:
- All eight keep the scale-set API, the workflow YAML and the `Required` checks as the merge gate, and reuse `velnor-runner-github`'s wire types.
- All eight put placement, warm capacity and caches in Velnor-owned Rust code, and none ports legacy's 37k-line `executor.rs` or `runner.rs`.
- Legacy's expression engine is worth taking, as code or as a test corpus: all except Gemini 4 Argon.
- Pre-seed the official runner's action archive cache: Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Fable 5.2, Muse Wonder, Gemini 4 Carbon, Gemini 4 Argon. Reviews: it saves the tarball download, not the resolution call (GPT-6.2-Astra, GPT-6.2-Sol).

Disagree:
- **Executor end state.** Official runner only (Gemini 4 Argon; the default for GPT-6.2-Astra and GPT-6.2-Sol), a native lane for the generated profile (Claude Fable 5.2, Muse Wonder, Muse Wonder Full; optional for Claude Opus 5.5), or a general native worker (Gemini 4 Carbon).
- **Seeing the job before placing it.** Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Fable 5.2 replace `Runner.Listener` so the dispatcher reads the job message first. Gemini 4 Argon classifies from the scale-set offer message and keeps the listener. GPT-6.2-Astra and GPT-6.2-Sol place by class only, because offers carry limited metadata. Reviews: seven reviewers confirm that an offer cannot bind a job to a runner, so only the first approach, or one scale set per cache class, gives placement. Gemini 4 Argon's own review now calls owning `acquirejob` before node selection the prerequisite.
- **velnor-new's journal and ownership proofs.** Keep (Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6.2-Astra, GPT-6.2-Sol, Muse Wonder, Muse Wonder Full, Gemini 4 Argon), drop for a crash-only agent (Claude Fable 5.2), or replace with a journal under 1,500 lines (Gemini 4 Carbon).
- **PR #1324.** Build on it (Muse Wonder, Muse Wonder Full, Gemini 4 Argon; GPT-6.2-Astra adopts its boundary). Change its unit and leave the journal behind (Claude Fable 5.2). Freeze the host for macOS and start clean (Gemini 4 Carbon). Claude Opus 5.5 rejects its one-volume-per-family cache; GPT-6.2-Sol delays the trait. Reviews: PR #1324 shards each node into K cache slots, so the one-volume reading is wrong (Gemini 4 Argon).
- **Falling back from the native lane.** Automatic: Claude Fable 5.2 (L2 to L1 under the same listener), Muse Wonder (reject before the first step and "send it to the official Worker"), Muse Wonder Full (admission before any side effect), Gemini 4 Carbon (a fleet-wide engine toggle). GPT-6.2-Astra and GPT-6.2-Sol: a consumed lease must not be transferred, so route unsupported jobs before acquisition and fail explicitly afterwards. Reviews side with the last two: the job body exists only after `acquirejob`, and a second acquire returns HTTP 409, so Muse Wonder Full's `admit()` cannot hand a job to an unmodified official runner (GPT-6.2-Sol, Gemini 4 Carbon).
- **A cache-protocol service derived from legacy `gha_cache.rs`.** Take (Claude Fable 5.2, Muse Wonder Full, Gemini 4 Argon) or skip (Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6.2-Sol; Gemini 4 Carbon writes its own). Reviews: the unmodified Worker overwrites an environment redirect on every step, so the service needs a listener that rewrites the job message (Claude Opus 5.5, Gemini 4 Carbon, Muse Wonder Full).

## Unique ideas

Ideas that only one or two models propose. Several did not survive review; the next section says which.

- **Claude Opus 5.5:** a job-level verdict key from the files the job read, recorded with fanotify; a trusted node builder as the only trusted cache writer, replacing the push run and the state seal; sandboxes pre-created the moment the plan job starts; agents enable auto-merge and stop "0 behind main" catch-up merges; split the 157k-line `velnor-runner-host` into 6–10 crates.
- **Claude Fable 5.2:** a crash-only node agent with no journal; gate jobs on the control-plane node; elastic cpusets on L3 groups; line budgets (dispatcher ≤8k, agent ≤15k, executor ≤12k); Landlock-enforced inputs ("Enforcement, not observation"); a test time budget (no test over 10 s) as a required check; dependencies at `opt-level = 3` in the test profile; push failures on already-verified SHAs as a flake metric (10 of 96).
- **GPT-6.2-Astra:** a dependency replay of real runs as the acceptance baseline (also GPT-6.2-Sol); a policy clock for the advisory database; tiny shards as worker groups inside one job; CPU-seconds and GiB-minutes in every comparison so larger pods cannot fake a win; the time before GitHub creates the first job as its own bucket (497 s in one run).
- **GPT-6.2-Sol:** one always-evaluated reducer in place of the two required jobs; a cache service instead of a shared writable host mount; same-repo PR and merge-group code treated as untrusted; "≥15% more verified jobs per hour" as a promotion metric; separate control planes per site.
- **Muse Wonder:** node labels that carry cache digests, matched by preferred affinity; the node agent signs shared cache generations with a cluster key; a non-required job that reports blaze-against-cargo agreement; the job-started hook timestamps the first cargo command.
- **Muse Wonder Full:** Plan and verdict as controller-native computations; an expression fuzzer against the official runner (≥1M cases) and a protocol-drift drill; a 20–30 s push debounce for agents; Docker checks on fork PRs deferred with a visibly reduced verdict.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon:** retire mbx for plain cargo over reflinked `target/` snapshots; KVM microVMs restored from a snapshot with dockerd already running; split ARC into no-dind and docker pools at once; squash the 52 journal migrations; replace hard-coded sleeps in `runner_image_qualification_public`; `renewjob` sent in parallel with setup; Spegel for image distribution.
- **Gemini 4 Argon:** copy `event.json` before background steps to avoid a truncation race; classify a job from the scale-set offer message; Sysbox in place of privileged dind; mbx as linker driver; choose `-p` or `--workspace` by comparing feature sets; `dockerd --mtu=1420`; delete the Ledger CLI job; a plan measured in days.
- **Shared by two:** official Worker under an own listener, and sampled re-verification with `MBX_VERIFY` (Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Fable 5.2); unsupported jobs routed before acquisition, hit rates with explicit denominators (GPT-6.2-Astra, GPT-6.2-Sol); pods reused across jobs (Muse Wonder, Muse Wonder Full); a hashed home node per cache group (Muse Wonder Full, Gemini 4 Argon); one test sweep for runner-host and local-mode, and no migration replay in journal tests, by squashing or a pre-migrated template (Gemini 4 Carbon, Gemini 4 Argon); no pod per job (Claude Fable 5.2, Gemini 4 Carbon); parallel golden tests (Claude Opus 5.5, Gemini 4 Carbon); line budgets for the new runner (Claude Fable 5.2, Muse Wonder Full); merge-queue minimum wait cut from 5 min to 0–1 (Muse Wonder Full, Gemini 4 Carbon); the org's Free plan named as the cause of the hosted queue (Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Fable 5.2).

Factual errors confirmed by more than one reviewer:
- **No 5-minute merge-queue floor.** The wait applies only while the minimum group size is unmet, and the minimum is 1. Five reviewers, against Gemini 4 Carbon's baseline and saving and Muse Wonder Full's cut to 0–1 min; Gemini 4 Carbon's own review withdraws the claim.
- **"Push-to-merge" figures that measure something else.** Gemini 4 Carbon adds run medians from different cohorts (GPT-6.2-Astra, Muse Wonder). Gemini 4 Argon relabels PR run wall time and models no merge stage (all three of its reviewers).
- **PR runs do not execute all 39 jobs.** The median is 25 executed, and the heavy jobs almost always run. Against Muse Wonder Full, Gemini 4 Argon and Muse Wonder's ×39 arithmetic (Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Fable 5.2, GPT-6.2-Sol). Muse Wonder's review calls the pack's 39 a different definition, not an error.
- **The 48/48 sources miss is not a key problem.** Rust jobs take the key from Plan's output (`ci.yml:671`). The restore path is built from `runner.temp`, which is `/home/runner/work/_temp` on hosted runners and `/home/runner/_work/_temp` on the scale set, and the cache version is derived from the path. Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Fable 5.2 and Gemini 4 Argon confirmed this in code; GPT-6.2-Astra and Muse Wonder accept it. Muse Wonder Full and GPT-6.2-Sol want both lanes' values printed from one run first. Compiled-state restores fail separately: that hash includes `ImageOS`, the store path and the checkout path.
- **mold is compared with the wrong linker.** Rust 1.99 links this target with rust-lld by default. Six reviewers, against Muse Wonder, Muse Wonder Full and Gemini 4 Argon.
- **`compiler_process_budget = 2` does not throttle crate jobs** (Claude Fable 5.2, Gemini 4 Carbon, against Muse Wonder Full and Gemini 4 Argon). The only `-j2` cap is `--config build.jobs=2` in the maintenance tasks, which also overrides `CARGO_BUILD_JOBS` (Gemini 4 Argon).
- **maintenance-helpers is not a compile job that a cache can shrink to 20–120 s.** Its tests alone take 212–246 s (three reviewers, against Muse Wonder and Gemini 4 Carbon).
- **The plain-cargo "0 crates" result is a lab result.** The #585 comment itself leaves plain cargo unverified at velnor-new's size (Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6.2-Astra).
- **A running pod cannot gain volumes or a new priority, and pods with unequal requests and limits get no pinned CPUs** (four reviewers, against Gemini 4 Argon, Muse Wonder Full and Muse Wonder).
- **Local-mode is not covered by two test binaries or by a default-feature `cargo check`.** The feature enables its own library tests and gates production code (five reviewers, against Gemini 4 Carbon and Gemini 4 Argon).
- **velnor-gen's golden workflow is not yet a drop-in.** It caches `target/` with plain cargo and has no mbx step, runs checks in sequence, omits the local-mode run and the lint jobs, and emits only `Required`, while the ruleset also requires `Required verdict` (Gemini 4 Carbon, Gemini 4 Argon; the golden file was re-checked for this document). All eight proposals cut over to it.

Claims the reviews overturned:
- **Warm-node placement has no mechanism while unmodified official Listeners hold the registrations.** `generatejitconfig` takes a runner name and a work folder, with no job id, and GitHub hands each job to a registered runner of its own choosing. Seven reviewers state this. It removes the mechanism behind the placement designs of Muse Wonder, Muse Wonder Full and Gemini 4 Argon (and Gemini 4 Carbon's coordinator, per Claude Opus 5.5), and behind the warm-hit rates that GPT-6.2-Astra (75%) and GPT-6.2-Sol (70–85%) assume under ARC. Reviewers name three ways out. One: a scale set or label per cache class, pinned to a node set (Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Fable 5.2; GPT-6.2-Astra says pool-level routing). Two: a Velnor listener that acquires first and then starts the official Worker on the chosen node, as Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Fable 5.2 proposed. Gemini 4 Carbon and Gemini 4 Argon back it, GPT-6.2-Astra would test it, and Muse Wonder Full warns that it reimplements "the fragile half of the official runner"; it needs a node-local shim, because the Worker reads `.runner` from disk and inherits local pipes. Three: warm every node from main, so that placement matters less (Claude Fable 5.2, GPT-6.2-Sol, Muse Wonder Full).
- **Lane selection at acquire time is too late.** The job body arrives only from the run-service `acquirejob` call, a second acquire returns HTTP 409, and no hand-off of an acquired job to another official Listener has been shown. So Muse Wonder Full's `admit()` cannot choose which runner gets a job (GPT-6.2-Sol, Gemini 4 Carbon). The "automatic" fallbacks of Claude Fable 5.2 and Gemini 4 Carbon cover only jobs that have not started: after a mid-job discovery the job fails (Muse Wonder, GPT-6.2-Astra). Routes the reviewers leave open: route unsupported jobs before acquisition, or acquire once and hand off to an official Worker before execution starts, which GPT-6.2-Sol wants proven first.
- **Single-test floors invalidate sub-minute targets and shard-division arithmetic.** All eight reviewers. One looped test sets about 4 min for any change that selects runner-host or the CLI, and a partition cannot split a test. This removes Gemini 4 Carbon's 24–38 s verdict, Gemini 4 Argon's 38 s test stage, Muse Wonder's 1.0 min shard and Muse Wonder Full's 35–50 s runner-host tests, and it binds GPT-6.2-Sol's 50–120 s test stage. Gemini 4 Argon's own review now says no architecture gets under 3.5–4.5 min until four code defects are fixed. Two reviewers also doubt Claude Opus 5.5's 7 s per split case, because each case may repeat the fixture cost.
- **No mechanism for reusing test results survived its reviewers.** Traced read sets (Claude Opus 5.5), a sandbox-enforced verdict cache (Claude Fable 5.2) and binary digests (Muse Wonder Full) were each judged unproven or unsound by all three of their reviewers. Tree-equal reuse of the PR verdict (Muse Wonder, Muse Wonder Full, Gemini 4 Carbon, Gemini 4 Argon) fits 7 of 45 sampled cases and does not show that the queue's checks ran (four reviewers). What reviewers accept: replacing the full push run on an identical SHA with a check of trusted merge evidence, and cross-event reuse that starts at 0%.
- **A local cache or artifact endpoint cannot be switched on by environment variable.** The official Worker sets `ACTIONS_CACHE_URL` and `ACTIONS_RESULTS_URL` from the job message on every step (Claude Opus 5.5, Gemini 4 Carbon, Muse Wonder Full). It needs a listener that rewrites the job message, a native worker, or interception. This affects the plans of Claude Fable 5.2 (first stage), Muse Wonder Full, Gemini 4 Carbon and Gemini 4 Argon.
- **Reusing a pod for the next job lowers isolation below today's** (six reviewers, against Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full). Pre-created single-use pods give the same gain; pod start is about 6 s.
- **A crash-only agent with no journal cannot report lost jobs** (GPT-6.2-Astra, Gemini 4 Carbon, against Claude Fable 5.2). Both suggest a small durable lease record instead of velnor-new's full journal.
- **Plan and verdict cannot become controller computations before Velnor owns the job protocol.** Only `completejob` can publish the outputs that later jobs read (Claude Fable 5.2, Gemini 4 Carbon, against Muse Wonder Full's Phase 2).
- **Zero-queue replays are lower bounds, not forecasts.** They hold cluster durations constant while the gate jobs and their load move onto the cluster (four reviewers, on GPT-6.2-Astra and GPT-6.2-Sol).
- **Most plans stop at the PR verdict.** Reviewers of six proposals find no merge-queue model or throughput target. The two that model the queue (Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Fable 5.2) are told that deeper speculation multiplies invalidated runs. Claude Fable 5.2 and Muse Wonder add that no plan sets a flake budget, although about one push run in ten failed on a commit that had already passed.

Where reviewers disagree with each other:
- **mbx or plain cargo.** Gemini 4 Carbon's review holds that "The repo's own evidence refutes all three on `mbx`". Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6.2-Astra and Muse Wonder call it unsettled until both are run on runner-host.
- **Replacing ARC.** Claude Fable 5.2 and GPT-6.2-Sol now call it unproven as a speed lever: what ARC lacks, persistence and warmth, is a node-local volume in the pod template and more idle runners. Gemini 4 Carbon and Gemini 4 Argon call a Velnor listener the prerequisite for placement.
- **Fail-safe lane admission.** Claude Fable 5.2 would adopt Muse Wonder Full's two lanes; GPT-6.2-Sol and Gemini 4 Carbon find that its admission runs too late.
- **Tree receipts.** Claude Opus 5.5 would adopt Gemini 4 Carbon's tree-verdict receipts for identical trees; GPT-6.2-Astra holds that a receipt for a partial PR plan can lack checks the queue requires.
- **The local cache endpoint.** Claude Fable 5.2 would adopt Gemini 4 Argon's; Muse Wonder Full finds it "not implementable as described".
- **The symlink fix for the sources path.** Claude Fable 5.2: it leaves `runner.temp` unchanged, so it fails. GPT-6.2-Sol: it "may preserve the spelled path".
- **Reviewer slips.** Muse Wonder Full calls PRs #1333 and #1349 "already-landed"; the pack lists them as open. Gemini 4 Argon says all three proposals it reviewed reject sccache; GPT-6.2-Astra proposes it as an alternative.
